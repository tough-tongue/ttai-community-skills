## 1. Format at a glance

| Item | 2026-2027 | Source and notes |
|---|---|---|
| Number of questions | **5** | [Official] Up from 3 last cycle |
| Prep time per question | **10 seconds** (most recent) | [Consultant] Vantage Point, updated Sept 2026. Kellogg's 2019 blog and Aringo/Leland say 20 seconds. Kellogg's current wording is "a brief moment" |
| Answer time | **Up to 60 seconds** | [Official] You can stop early; recording cuts off at the limit |
| Who asks | A recorded prompt from an admissions team member | [Official] Lance Bennett, 2019 |
| Total time | About 30 minutes including setup | [Official] |
| When it unlocks | After application submission and payment, via Application Status Page | [Official] Questions are not shared in advance |
| Deadline | **96 hours after the round deadline** | [Official] R1: Sept 9, 2026. R2: Jan 6, 2027 |

### What changed this cycle
Kellogg replaced two 450-word essays and the short-answer goals questions with one 550-word two-part written essay, and expanded video from 3 to 5 questions. [Official]

Kellogg's stated reason: a lower-stress way to share your story that gives the team more authentic, in-the-moment insight. [Official, via SBC]

Consultant read (SBC, Stratus): every top-school adcom at the 2026 AIGAC conference was talking about making admissions more holistic and authentic in an era of AI-sounding essays. Kellogg leaned on video because it is hard to fake. SBC: the video essays now "carry real weight."

## 2. Why Kellogg does this, in Kellogg's own words

This section matters because it tells you what the reviewer is actually trying to do when they hit play.

**The gap it fills.** Kellogg tries to interview every applicant, but alumni and current students conduct most of those interviews. So the admissions team itself rarely meets you. Kellogg describes itself as relationship-focused; the video lets the team see the face and hear the voice behind the file they just read. [Official] Lance Bennett, Director of Diversity Admissions, 2019

**The whole team gets a candid look.** When you interview with one alum or one officer, the rest of the committee never sees you unfiltered. The video gives everyone the same candid view. [Adcom] Kate Smith, Assistant Dean of Admissions, interview with Menlo Coaching

**It mirrors recruiting.** Kellogg's Career Management Center sees firms like Goldman, Uber, Apple and Google using video interviews in hiring. The video essay is partly practice for that. [Official] 2019 blog; [Adcom] Kate Smith

**They wanted to see and hear applicants.** A Kellogg rep at an mbaMission conference said the component grew out of a simple wish to actually see and hear the people they were evaluating. [Adcom, via mbaMission]

**They do not expect a performer.** Kellogg knows applicants are not comfortable on camera in a high-stakes setting and does not expect polish. [Adcom] Kate Smith. mbaMission's paraphrase: they are not looking for a TED speaker or a late-night host.

**Their four tips, verbatim in spirit.** Be yourself. Use the practice questions to get used to the technology, and polish the one question everyone gets. Make sure you will not be interrupted, because once you start you cannot restart. Smile and enjoy it. [Official] Lance Bennett, 2019

## 3. What is being measured

### Kellogg's own framing
The video exists to "showcase your personality and share some of the experiences that brought you here." Kellogg says it wants to see the person behind the carefully crafted words. [Official]

Kellogg's broader application lens: leaders with a blend of analytical, creative and social intelligence who can lift teams. The review is holistic and there are "no perfect answers." [Official] 2023 application tips post

Kellogg's three foundational filters, per Kate Smith: academic rigor, commitment to diversity (ready to be challenged by it and to seek it out), and high engagement (committed to real impact). The video is mostly read against the last two. [Adcom]

### Consultant consensus on what a reviewer notices

| Dimension | What a strong answer shows | Who says so |
|---|---|---|
| Communication clarity | Direct answer up front, one story, lands inside 60 seconds | Aringo, Leland, Vantage Point |
| Thinking on your feet | Organizes fast with almost no prep time; spontaneity is the design goal | Aringo, Vantage Point, mbaMission |
| Authenticity | Unscripted, not memorized, not AI-polished; recovers from a stumble with a laugh | SBC, mbaMission, Aringo, Leland, Menlo |
| Personality and warmth | Energy, smile, eye contact; a personality check, not a performance | Aringo, mbaMission, Vantage Point |
| Leadership potential | Initiative, bringing people together, deciding under uncertainty | Leland, Aringo |
| Teamwork and Kellogg fit | Low-ego, "we" over "I", impact plus humility ("Kellogg DNA") | SBC, Aringo, Leland, Ellin Lolis |
| Self-awareness and growth | Owns failure, shows what changed | Leland, Aringo |
| Values | Decisions guided by something deeper than results | Aringo |
| Consistency with the file | Complements the written essay, never repeats or contradicts it | Vantage Point, Leland, Aringo |
| Presence | Eye contact with the lens, steady pace, posture, sound, light | Leland, Vantage Point |

### What hurts
- Memorized or over-polished delivery. SBC and Leland both say readers can tell. Menlo's trick: if you catch yourself reciting, change your first sentence and see how much it throws you.
- Reusing essay anecdotes word for word
- Long setup, vague outcome, jumping between stories
- No takeaway or reflection at the end
- Blaming others in a failure story
- Reading notes, flat tone, looking at the screen instead of the lens
- Ignoring the intro question or treating it as a resume recap

## 4. The question bank

Kellogg does not publish the bank. What follows is layered from most to least authoritative.

### 4A. What Kellogg has published [Official]

When Kellogg used a three-question format, it told applicants the structure and even gave two of the questions:

1. **Introduce yourself to the admissions committee.** "Consider this your opportunity to share what you would want your future Kellogg classmates and our admissions committee to know about you. What makes you, you?" (2019)
2. **What path are you interested in pursuing, how will you get there, and why is this program right for you?** Described as intentionally broad so you can answer honestly. (2016, 2019)
3. **A randomly selected interview-style prompt**, all with the same objective. Kellogg's own example: "Tell me about a time you were not going to be able to meet a deadline. How did you handle the situation? What was the outcome?" (2016)

Kellogg's original FAQ (archived by Aringo) said the same: one icebreaker, one future-plans-and-Kellogg question, the rest random.

**Working assumption for the five-question format:** the intro and the goals/why-Kellogg slots are still fixed, and three random behavioral or personal prompts fill the rest. Kellogg has not confirmed this for 2026-2027. Vantage Point confirms the intro question is still asked of everyone.

### 4B. Candidate-reported questions [Reported]

Compiled on GMAT Club by moderator Sajjad1994 (January 2026) from applicants in the 2025-2026 cycle. Treat wording as approximate. This list is the closest thing to ground truth and includes prompts the consultants do not list.

**Personal background and self-reflection**
1. Introduce yourself / tell us something not on your resume
2. What are your values and how will you bring them to Kellogg? / What unique value do you bring to your professional setting?
3. A misconception your co-workers have about you; given the chance, how would you correct it?
4. What prepared you for graduate-level education outside of work?

**Adaptability and resilience**
5. A time you stepped out of your comfort zone; what steps did you take and what did you learn?
6. A time you had to accommodate a last-minute request or an unplanned task
7. A time in the last year you solved a problem in a creative or innovative way
8. What is your greatest fear, and how are you working on it?
9. If you could change one decision in your past, what would it be?

**Conflict, feedback and collaboration**
10. A situation where you were the cause of conflict on your team; how did you recognize and resolve it?
11. A decision that went against your team's opinion; how did you handle pushback?
12. How you resolved a conflict between two people or handled differing viewpoints
13. A time you made a team more inclusive or ensured everyone felt valued
14. Feedback you disagreed with; how did you react and respond?
15. Advice you received, how you implemented it, and the impact

**Leadership, impact and work style**
16. What professional environment or culture do you thrive in and why?
17. A leader you admire or aspire to be like, and why
18. A time you created something of value or improved a process at work
19. A positive impact on an organization or community; why was it significant?
20. A time you failed and had to change your approach; how did you correct course?
21. A task you could not achieve alone; how did you identify that and what did you do?

**Motivation, goals and fit**
22. There are many top MBA programs. What draws you to Kellogg?
23. How long have you been considering an MBA? What have you discovered about yourself during the process?
24. Why an MBA? How will Kellogg help you?

**Networking and relationships**
25. A connection you made that benefited you; who was it and how did it help?
26. How you leveraged a relationship or connection to achieve a goal

### 4D. Pattern summary
Across all three tiers the same seven buckets recur. If you can answer one question well from each, you are covered:

| Bucket | Fixed or random | Prep priority |
|---|---|---|
| Introduce yourself / not on resume | Fixed, everyone gets it | Highest. Kellogg says to polish this one |
| Why MBA / why Kellogg / your path | Likely fixed | High |
| Team conflict or feedback | Random | High |
| Failure and course correction | Random | High |
| Leadership or impact story | Random | Medium |
| Values, admired person, work style | Random | Medium |
| Curveball (fear, extra hour, past decision) | Random | Low, but practice thinking fast |

## 5. How to prepare

### Build a story bank
- Aringo: 6 to 8 core stories, each in Situation-Action-Result form
- SBC: 2 to 3 personal stories that show values, resilience or teamwork
- Vantage Point: pick the best stories that did not fit the written essay
- Leland: choose moments with a real setback or a real decision, not generic wins
- Cross-check the seven buckets in 4D. Each story should be reusable across at least two buckets.

### Structure every answer for 60 seconds
Leland's five-part template:
1. One sentence that directly answers the prompt
2. Brief context (role, team, situation)
3. What you specifically did
4. Result
5. What you learned and how it shapes you now

Sam Weeks: SCAR (Situation, Challenge, Action, Result) plus one reflection line. Both fit in 60 seconds if the context is two sentences at most.

### Practice the skill, not the script
- Practice out loud with a timer. Running long is the most common failure; most first-timers get cut off. (Vantage Point)
- Record and watch back for filler words, tics and flat tone. (SBC, Leland)
- Have a friend fire random prompts from Section 4 at you. (SBC)
- Use Kellogg's portal practice mode; it mirrors the real thing. (Official)
- Polish only the intro answer. Everything else should be structured on the fly. (Official, Lance Bennett)
- If you notice you are reciting, change the first sentence to break the pattern. (Menlo)

### Delivery
- Look at the webcam, not the screen. Speak slowly. Smile. (Vantage Point)
- Camera at eye level, light in front of you, clean background, USB mic if you have one. (Leland)
- Dress as you would for an interview. (Leland)
- If you stumble, laugh or shrug and keep going. Grace reads better than rigidity. (mbaMission)
- You do not have to fill the full minute. Stop when you are done. (Official)

### Content rules
- Complement the written essay; do not repeat it. (Vantage Point, Aringo)
- Have fun with the intro: hobbies, family, pets are all fair game. (Vantage Point)
- Tie "why Kellogg" to specifics: clubs, courses, MMM, global programs, conversations with students. (Leland)
- Kellogg allows AI as a sounding board but requires authorship to be yours. A scripted or AI-polished video defeats the point. (Official, via SBC)

## 6. Self-assessment rubric

Score each 1 to 5. Derived from Section 3. Works for a mock evaluator too.

1. Answered the question directly in the first sentence
2. One clear story with situation, action, result
3. Finished inside 60 seconds without being cut off or padding
4. Stated a reflection or takeaway
5. Sounded natural (no script cadence, recovered from stumbles)
6. Warmth and energy (smile, eye contact, vocal variety)
7. Kellogg fit signals (collaboration, humility, impact on others)
8. New information not already in the resume or essay
9. Self-awareness (owned mistakes, showed growth)
10. Technical quality (audio, lighting, framing)

## 7. Open questions and caveats

- **Prep time.** 10 vs 20 seconds is unresolved. Kellogg's own 2019 post said 20; Vantage Point's September 2026 update says 10. Plan for 10.
- **Five-slot structure.** Kellogg has not confirmed which slots are fixed. The intro appears fixed; the rest is inference from the old three-question format.
- **Question wording.** All reported prompts are recalled by applicants after the fact. Expect variation.
- **Currency.** GMAT Club's list is from the 2025-2026 cycle. R1 videos for 2026-2027 were due around Sept 13, 2026, so fresh five-question reports should appear on GMAT Club, r/MBA and Clear Admit LiveWire within a week or two. Worth re-checking.
- Clear Admit's essay analysis page still says "three questions" and is out of date on that point.
