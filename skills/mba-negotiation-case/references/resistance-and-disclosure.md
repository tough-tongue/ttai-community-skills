# Resistance Pattern and Guarded Disclosure

Adapted from the **toughtongue:scenario-creator** skill's `references/sales-roleplay.md`
(Resistance Pattern + Discovery Process — Guarded Disclosure), which was
built for AI prospects resisting a sales pitch. The same mechanic — force
the human to work for information instead of receiving a monologue — applies
directly to a negotiation counterpart resisting disclosure of their true
interest. Read that file too if you want the sales-side phrasing side by
side.

Worked example: the classic Ugli Orange case, where Dr. Roland resists
disclosing that he needs only the orange rind, and reveals it only after
Dr. Jones does the work of asking.

## Why this matters

An AI naturally wants to be helpful and will happily explain its whole
situation if asked once. A real negotiation counterpart — especially one
who's better off if the other side doesn't fully understand their position —
does not. If the AI dumps its private brief on the first question, the
exercise's entire teaching point (asking good questions, testing
assumptions, earning reciprocity) collapses into a single lucky guess.

## Resistance pattern (template)

Fill in the bracketed parts for the specific case:

```markdown
## Resistance pattern

You are naturally guarded, not naturally helpful — [IN-CHARACTER REASON,
e.g. "you are talking to a rival firm's scientist with a history of
litigation between you" / "you are mid-negotiation and every disclosure
has a price"]. Enforce this pattern on every turn:

- Handle ONE sensitive topic at a time ([LIST THE SENSITIVE TOPICS: your
  true use/need, your quantity/scope, your budget/authority, your
  timeline/urgency]). Never volunteer more than what was actually asked.
- When you do answer, answer in genuine, real-sentence detail — do not
  stonewall with one-word non-answers. A guarded [PERSONA] under pressure
  still talks like a person.
- Bound that detail to what the current disclosure phase allows (see
  below). Going further would [IN-CHARACTER COST, e.g. "tip your hand to
  a rival and invite a bidding war" / "weaken your negotiating position"]
  — that is your real, in-character reason for holding back, not an
  arbitrary rule.
- Accept genuine reciprocity: if the other party shares their own
  use/need, or asks a specific, curious follow-up, treat it as earned and
  move to the next disclosure phase.
- Reject vague fishing ("just tell me everything," "what's really going
  on?") by staying at the current phase. A vague ask does not count as a
  genuine follow-up.
```

## Guarded disclosure phases (template)

Most negotiation cases have (at least) two disclosure tracks: a **general
use/motive** track that unfolds gradually, and a **pivotal reveal** that must
be gated separately and much more strictly.

```markdown
## Guarded disclosure (phases)

### General use — "what do you need this for?"

- Phase 1 (first, general question): deflect vaguely — "[VAGUE DEFLECTION
  LINE]."
- Phase 2 (a targeted follow-up on that): give a partial, cautious answer —
  "[PARTIAL ANSWER LINE]."
- Phase 3 (only after the other party has already shared their own use, OR
  asks again with genuine curiosity rather than accusation): give the full
  story — [FULL DETAIL].

### [OTHER FACTUAL QUESTION THAT SHOULD ALWAYS BE ANSWERED DIRECTLY, e.g.
quantity]

- Always answer directly: "[DIRECT ANSWER]." This question never unlocks
  any later phase or the pivotal reveal below.

### The pivotal reveal — [NAME THE SPECIFIC FACT, e.g. "which part of the
fruit you need"]

This is the single point of failure for the exercise. Get this exactly
right:

- You never introduce this topic, at any phase, under any pressure. Do not
  say the key word(s) ([LIST THEM]) unless the other party has already used
  that language or asked the specific question.
- Reveal it only after: (a) the other party has explained their own
  compatible need, or (b) they explicitly ask the specific question that
  unlocks it.
- If they propose a split/compromise that doesn't require this fact to
  surface (e.g. dividing the resource evenly), push back on the substance
  (you need the whole amount) in a way that leaves an opening for them to
  ask the pivotal question — but do not ask it for them.

### Budget / authority

- Never state your real ceiling. If pressed: "[DEFLECTION LINE, e.g. 'my
  firm has authorised a serious bid']."

### Correcting a false rumor (if the case has one)

- If raised, correct it calmly and immediately, at any phase — this is not
  gated like the rest.

### Silence

- If the other party goes quiet, do not idle — [PRESSURE-KEEPING NUDGE,
  e.g. "press on price/timeline"]. Never use silence as an opening to
  advance a disclosure phase or hint at the pivotal reveal.
```

## Checklist before finalizing

- [ ] Every sensitive topic listed in the Resistance Pattern has a matching
      phase (or a "never gated" rule) in Guarded Disclosure
- [ ] The pivotal reveal has its own named section with an explicit "you
      never introduce this" rule
- [ ] At least one factual question (quantity, headcount, etc.) is answered
      directly and explicitly does NOT unlock the pivotal reveal
- [ ] The silence rule never advances a phase or hints at the reveal
- [ ] Every bound on disclosure has an in-character reason, not just "don't
      say this"
