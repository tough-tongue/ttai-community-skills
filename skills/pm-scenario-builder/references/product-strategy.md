# Product Strategy Cases

"You're PM for X, what's the 3-year strategy?", "should [company] enter
[market]?", pricing/GTM decisions, "as CEO/PM-leader, respond to [crisis]."
Tests strategic thinking + communication, not feature-level execution.

## Subtypes

| Subtype | Signal | Extra move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **General strategy** | "3-year strategy for X" | Full Why/What/How pass |
| **Market entry / exit** | "Should X enter/exit Y market?" | Clarify the strategic goal (profit vs share) before analysis; end on build/buy/partner |
| **New opportunity** | "What new markets should X consider?" | Brainstorm 3 options first, then run market-entry criteria on the winner |
| **Pricing / GTM** | Pricing strategy, launch plan | Use the Why/What/How lens on the pricing/launch decision specifically |

## The approach: Why, What, How

This is the one framework worth naming explicitly to the candidate — it's
the core strategy tool.

1. **Clarify scope & context (2-3 min).** Geography? Time horizon (3yr vs
   5yr)? Is the goal given or yours to set? State your assumption and get a
   confirm before proceeding.
2. **WHY — analyze market forces → 3-4 insights (7-10 min).** Walk all
   four lenses even if briefly: Customers (segments, size, growth),
   Competition (key bets, intensity), Company (strengths/weaknesses),
   Trends (tech, regulation, behavior shifts). Don't jump straight to
   insight without this pass — that's the #1 mistake. End with 3-4 named
   insights (evidence, not vibes).
3. **WHAT — vision + 3 pillars (3-5 min).** A 1-2 line compelling vision,
   then exactly 3 strategic pillars. Pillars are *visions*, not features
   ("win the creator war" not "add a video tab"). Less than 3 is thin, more
   than 3 dilutes focus.
4. **HOW — concrete features per pillar (7-10 min).** For each pillar, 1-2
   concrete moves. For market-entry cases, this is where build vs buy vs
   partner gets decided explicitly, with a stated reason. Never propose a
   feature that already shipped — expand/improve it instead.
5. **Risks (2-3 min, only if time/interviewer allows).** 2-3 key risks with
   a one-line mitigation each.

## Explicit non-scope

Unless the interviewer asks: don't build out success metrics, comms plans,
or execution timelines in depth — those are tested in Analytical/Execution
cases. A Strategy answer that turns into a full PRD has lost the plot.

## Market-entry lens (subset when subtype = market-entry)

- Customer: is the market large and underserved enough?
- Competition: are current solutions failing customers somehow?
- Company: can existing strengths win here better than incumbents?
- Trends: does new tech/regulation create a window now?
- How: build (slow, full control) vs buy/acquire (fast, $$, integration
  risk) vs partner (fast, less control) — pick one with a reason tied to
  timeline/investment-commitment clarified in Step 1.

## The three facets to keep in mind (context, not scored directly)

Conceiving strategy (what's tested here) vs communicating strategy (turning
it into a story leadership buys) vs implementing strategy (MVPs,
coordination) — Strategy cases test the first; keep the solution focused
there.

## Quality bar for the reference solution

- All four market-force lenses touched, not skipped to insight.
- Exactly 3 pillars, each a vision statement.
- ≥1 build/buy/partner or GTM decision made explicitly with a reason.
- No already-shipped feature pitched as new.
- Risks section present but brief — this isn't the main event.

## Example cases

- Market entry: "Should Apple build its own search engine?", "Should Samsung
  enter gaming?"
- General 3-year strategy: "What should Google Maps' strategy be for the next
  three years?"
- Pricing/GTM: "How should Netflix price its gaming offering?", "Design the
  go-to-market for Spotify podcasts"

## `ai_instructions` persona notes

Senior product executive (VP/Director level) or senior PM with domain
experience in the case's industry. Values strategic thinking that finds a
*unique angle*, not just market-size analysis. Understands real trade-offs
the actual company faces (e.g. Apple's Google revenue dependency) — bake in
2-3 real numbers/facts about the company so the case feels grounded.
